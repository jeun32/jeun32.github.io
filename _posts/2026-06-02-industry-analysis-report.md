## 웨이퍼 워피지로 인한 메모리 반도체 수율 저하와 BSD(Back Side Deposition) 장비의 중요성 (한화투자증권, 2026.06.02)
This report highlights a growing concern in the semiconductor industry: **wafer warpage**, or the irregular bending of wafers. This phenomenon is significantly impacting the yield of memory semiconductors, particularly **HBM4** (High Bandwidth Memory) and **NAND flash**.

**Key Findings and Insights:**

*   **Problem:** Wafer warpage is causing a substantial decrease in the production yield of HBM4 (both base and core dies) and NAND. This issue is not limited to a single manufacturer but is observed across all three major DRAM players (Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron Technology).
*   **Causes of Warpage:**
    *   **HBM4:**
        *   **Die Thinning:** To achieve higher bandwidth and capacity, HBM4 requires more stacked layers, necessitating thinner individual DRAM dies. Thinner dies are more susceptible to bending from external forces and internal stress.
        *   **Increased Stress Factor Density:** HBM4 has more I/O, denser connection pitches, and more tightly packed structures (Cu in TSV, insulation layers, microbumps, base die wiring). These materials have different thermal expansion coefficients, leading to accumulated stress during repeated heating and cooling processes.
    *   **NAND:**
        *   **High Stacking (3D NAND):** As 3D NAND layers increase (hundreds of layers), the accumulated stress from the thin films (oxide, nitride) deposited on the wafer causes significant warpage. Each thin film exerts a small amount of stress, but when hundreds of layers are stacked, these small stresses accumulate in the same direction, leading to severe bending.
*   **Impact:** Wafer warpage directly affects the profitability of memory manufacturers and can lead to increased costs and delayed deliveries for customers.
*   **Solution: BSD (Back Side Deposition) Equipment:**
    *   **Function:** BSD equipment addresses warpage by depositing a compensatory film on the back side of the wafer. This film applies counter-stress to offset the stress generated by the films on the front side, effectively straightening the wafer.
    *   **Precision:** BSD is not just about applying a film; it requires precise control over the thickness and stress of the compensatory film based on the specific warpage profile of each wafer. This involves carefully adjusting gas composition, temperature, plasma intensity, and deposition time to create a film with the exact desired properties.
    *   **Growing Importance:** BSD equipment is expected to become increasingly crucial across HBM, NAND, and logic wafers. This is because all advanced semiconductor technologies are moving towards thinner wafers, more complex structures, higher stacking, and finer connections, all of which exacerbate warpage issues.
*   **Market Outlook:** The demand for BSD equipment is projected to increase significantly. Current estimates suggest a need for 1 unit per 2.5-3K NAND wafers and 1 unit per 5-7K DRAM/HBM wafers. With memory makers accelerating their expansion plans and increasing production scales, the adoption of BSD equipment is expected to rise, benefiting manufacturers of these tools.

**Conclusion:**

The report concludes with a **positive outlook** on the semiconductor industry, specifically highlighting the critical role of **BSD equipment** in addressing the pervasive wafer warpage problem. As semiconductor technology advances towards thinner, denser, and more complex designs, BSD will be essential for maintaining yield, supporting HBM bonding, enabling high-stack NAND, and ensuring the stability of logic micro-processes.
